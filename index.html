1 · Subject & Object Pronouns

RESOURCE EMPHASIS

This topic needs extra development here. The reference book does not provide a full basic review of subject and object pronouns, so this resource gives Álvaro the complete system needed for exam questions.

What job does the pronoun have?

A pronoun replaces a noun so we do not repeat the same person or thing. The key is not only the form, but the role it plays in the sentence.

A subject pronoun performs the action. An object pronoun receives the action or follows a preposition.

SUBJECT + VERB + OBJECT
She called him.

She performs the action, so it is the subject. Him receives the action, so it is the object.

PersonSubjectObjectExample
IImeI study. / The teacher called me.
youyouyouYou work. / I spoke to you.
hehehimHe arrived. / I saw him.
shesheherShe called. / I invited her.
itititIt works. / I need it.
weweusWe finished. / They helped us.
theytheythemThey left. / I met them.
BEFORE THE VERB

Use a subject pronoun when it does the action.

They study English.
AFTER A VERB

Use an object pronoun when it receives the action.

I called them.
AFTER A PREPOSITION

Use an object pronoun after to, for, with, from, etc.

She spoke to him.
✓ María and I study together.
✓ The teacher spoke to María and me.
✗ María and me study together.
✗ The teacher spoke to María and I.
Exam strategy: Remove the other name. “The teacher spoke to ___.” → “The teacher spoke to me.” This makes the correct form easier to identify.

2 · Possessive Adjectives & Pronouns

Both show possession — but they do different jobs.

A possessive adjective must be followed by a noun because it identifies the owner of that noun. A possessive pronoun replaces the noun completely.

possessive adjective + NOUN → my book / her laptop / their house
possessive pronoun ALONE → mine / hers / theirs
PersonPossessive adjectivePossessive pronounExample pair
Imyminemy phone / The phone is mine.
youyouryoursyour bag / The bag is yours.
hehishishis keys / The keys are his.
sheherhersher notebook / The notebook is hers.
weouroursour room / The room is ours.
theytheirtheirstheir car / The car is theirs.
Common traps: hers laptop, the laptop is her, mine book. These forms mix the adjective and pronoun patterns.
Fast check: If a noun appears immediately after the blank, use my/your/his/her/our/their. If the noun is already understood and not repeated, use mine/yours/his/hers/ours/theirs.

3 · Demonstratives: this / that / these / those

RESOURCE EMPHASIS

This topic is also developed more fully here. The official exam syllabus includes demonstratives, so Álvaro needs a clear decision system based on number and distance.

Two decisions control the answer.

First decide whether the noun is singular or plural. Then decide whether it is conceptually near or far from the speaker.

1Singular or plural?
2Near or far?
3Choose the form
NearFar
Singularthis bookthat book
Pluralthese booksthose books
THIS

One thing near.

This laptop is mine.
THESE

More than one thing near.

These files are ready.
THAT / THOSE

Use for things farther away.

Those buildings are new.
Do not mix number: this books ✗ / these book ✗.
Exam strategy: Look at the noun first. If it is plural, eliminate this and that immediately.

4 · Possessive Nouns: 's / s' / of

Possession can also be shown with nouns.

Use 's after a singular owner. If a plural noun already ends in -s, usually add only the apostrophe. With objects, places, or longer noun phrases, of is also common.

the manager's office
the students' laptops
the door of the building
✓ Álvaro's notebook
✓ the teachers' room
✗ Álvaros notebook
✗ the teacher's room (if several teachers share it)

5 · Countable & Noncount Nouns

Why does noun type matter?

The count/noncount distinction controls articles, plural forms, and quantifiers. Before choosing a/an, many, much, few, little, identify the noun type.

Countable nouns

Individual items that can be counted.

one chair → two chairs
a question → three questions

They can use a/an in the singular and many/a few in the plural.

Noncount nouns

Things treated as a mass, concept, material, or category.

information · advice · furniture · equipment · money

They normally do not use a/an and do not take plural -s.

Typical exam traps: an information, advices, equipments, many money.
Correct patterns: some information · a piece of advice · much money · a lot of equipment.

6 · Singular & Plural Nouns

Plural formation is not always just +s.

Most nouns add -s, but spelling changes and irregular forms are common enough to appear as distractors.

Regular

book → books
car → cars

-es / spelling change

box → boxes
city → cities
wife → wives

Irregular

person → people
child → children
man → men

Exam strategy: Check subject–verb agreement too. There is one box but There are two boxes.

7 · Articles: a / an / the / zero article

Articles show how the speaker identifies a noun.

a/an introduces one nonspecific singular count noun. the identifies something specific or already known. Zero article is common with general plural/noncount nouns, languages, subjects, and many meals.

FormCore ideaExample
aone nonspecific singular count noun + consonant sounda report · a university
anone nonspecific singular count noun + vowel soundan exam · an hour
thespecific / identifiable / unique in contextthe report on my desk
—general plural/noncount nouns; many general usesInformation is useful. / I study English.

a or an? Listen to the sound.

The choice depends on pronunciation, not just the first letter.

✓ a university /juː.../
✓ an hour /aʊ.../
✗ an university
✗ a hour
Exam strategy: Before choosing an article, ask: Is the noun countable? Singular? Specific? Already known?

8 · There is / There are

Use these structures to say that something exists or is present.

Choose the verb according to the noun that follows.

There is + singular / noncount noun
There are + plural noun

Singular / noncount

There is a bank near here.
There is some information online.

Question: Is there...?
Negative: There isn't...

Plural

There are two buses outside.
There are many questions.

Question: Are there...?
Negative: There aren't...

Do not confuse there with it. “There is a problem” introduces existence. “It is serious” refers back to the problem.
There is a problem. It is serious.
